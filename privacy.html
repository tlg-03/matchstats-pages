MatchStats — Privacy Policy

Last updated 13 November 2026

MatchStats apps collect no personal information. Nothing you enter ever reaches us.

There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking of any kind, and no third-party services. The apps contact no server, ours or anyone else's. Data leaves a device only when you send it yourself (a backup or report you save or share, or a live share on game day, below), or in your device's own iCloud or computer backup if you have one turned on.

Where your data lives

Everything you enter — games, team sheets, player names and numbers, coded events, notes, your club badge, any player photos and your settings — is stored on your own device, inside the app's own storage.

The apps also keep an automatic backup of your library on the same device, updated as you work: one file for each day you use the app, with the twelve most recent kept, so a lost or damaged library can be restored. On iPhone and iPad it is in the app's folder in the Files app (and in your device's own iCloud or computer backup, if you use one); on a Mac it is inside the app's own folder in your user Library (and in your Time Machine backup, if you use one).

Backups and reports you save yourself are ordinary files that go wherever you choose: the Files app, iCloud Drive, a folder on your Mac, or wherever you share them. Those copies are yours, and where they go and who can see them is under your control. On a Mac, a report file you have linked to a match is kept up to date as you code it.

If something goes wrong, the app notes the error in a short log kept on the device. It is never sent anywhere; you can copy it and email it to us if you want help.

Live share on game day

Where an app offers it, an analyst can share the match being coded with coaches' iPhones, iPads or Macs on the same Wi-Fi network, for example a box at the venue. It is off unless the analyst turns it on for an open match, and it stops when they end it or leave the match.

  • What is sent: the open match, with its team names, competition, grade, date and venue, the players' names, numbers and positions, every coded event and any notes. Badges and player photos are not sent.
  • Who receives it: only devices that can reach the analyst's device on the local network and enter the PIN the analyst set. It goes straight from the analyst's device to theirs over the local network: never through a server, never over mobile data, and never to us.
  • What others on the Wi-Fi can see: while a session is live, the match title (the two team names) is announced on that network so coaches can find it.
  • What the coaches keep: nothing. The match is shown, never saved into their library, and it is gone when they leave, unless a coach prints a report or saves it as a PDF.
  • Security: the PIN keeps the match from casual viewers. The app does not add its own encryption, so the protection of the data in transit is the Wi-Fi's own: use a network with a password that only your staff have.

The first time live share is used, the device asks for Local Network access. You can decline it; only live share stops working.

Camera, photos and images

On iPhone and iPad the apps ask for the camera only when you take a picture yourself. A picture from your library comes through the system photo picker, which gives the app only the picture you choose. Where the microphone is listed, it is only because the camera can film video. On a Mac nothing is asked: you choose an image file instead. Pictures are used for:

  • Club badge — the app reads its colours to work out the app's accent colour.
  • Team sheet import — the app reads the names off a photo of a printed team sheet (or, where offered, a team-list PDF).
  • Player photos, where offered — a small, cropped picture shown with a player's name.

All of this happens on the device; the text recognition is built into the app. No picture is ever uploaded, and a picture leaves the device only inside a backup or report you share yourself. A badge or player photo you choose is kept in your own saved data and your backups. The app does not keep a team-sheet photo or team-list PDF: it reads the names and discards the file.

You can decline these permissions and keep using everything else.

Children

MatchStats apps are tools for coaches and team staff. They collect no information from anyone, including children. Player names stay on the device they were entered on and in its backups. They leave it only in a file you save or share yourself, or on the screens of the coaches you share a match with during a live share.
